# How to Automate an Accounting Practice: 10 Workflows You Can Start With

The best accounting automation projects are usually ordinary processes that happen hundreds of times a year. Enquiry acknowledgement, appointment coordination, onboarding, document requests and recurring workflow setup may not look strategic on their own, but together they consume expensive professional capacity.

The right approach is to automate the repetitive steps while keeping judgement, review and client advice with the right person.

## 1. New enquiry response

Capture website and campaign enquiries in one system. Send an immediate acknowledgement, record the source and notify the right person. Where appropriate, collect basic qualification information before a consultation.

## 2. Appointment booking

Reduce email back-and-forth by connecting qualified prospects to available appointments. Automate confirmations and reminders while keeping escalation options for unusual requests.

## 3. Lead follow-up

A prospect who does not book immediately should not disappear into an inbox. Create a short, useful follow-up sequence with clear stop rules when the person responds or opts out.

## 4. Client onboarding

Build a defined path from accepted engagement to active client. Track forms, identity checks, documents, meetings and internal handovers. Make incomplete steps visible.

## 5. Document requests

Create standard request lists for common jobs. Record what has been requested, what has arrived and what remains outstanding.

## 6. Document reminders

Use agreed reminder timing instead of asking staff to remember every follow-up. Escalate exceptions to a person when repeated reminders do not resolve the issue.

## 7. Recurring work

For repeatable services, create standard workflow templates with owners, due dates, dependencies and checkpoints. This improves consistency across the team.

## 8. Internal notifications

Notify staff when a client completes a form, supplies a document, books a meeting or reaches a workflow stage that requires human action.

## 9. Client status communication

Approved status messages can reduce avoidable "where is this up to?" enquiries. Keep communications factual and tied to real workflow events.

## 10. Review and re-engagement

After suitable completed work, a practice can request feedback or a review. Dormant clients can also be placed into permission-based re-engagement workflows where appropriate.

## How to choose the first automation

Score each workflow on frequency, staff time, error risk and ease of standardisation. A high-frequency task with clear rules is usually a better first project than a complex process full of exceptions.

Document the current process before changing it. Record who performs each step, what system is used, what information is needed and what causes delays.

## Build human checkpoints

Automation should not make professional decisions on behalf of the firm. Define where staff approval is required. Sensitive, unusual or advice-related matters should move to an appropriately qualified person.

## Measure before and after

Track useful measures such as response time, number of manual touches, days to complete onboarding, outstanding document age and staff time spent on follow-up.

If the process does not improve, change the workflow rather than adding more automation.

## Put a business case around each workflow

Treat each automation as a small investment decision. Estimate the current annual effort, the expected reduction in manual touches, the implementation cost and the ongoing support requirement. Then compare the benefit with the risks and dependencies.

For a practice manager or finance leader, useful measures include:

- staff hours spent per month on the process
- average cycle time from trigger to completion
- number of overdue or stalled items
- rework caused by incomplete information
- write-offs or non-billable administration
- client complaints or repeated status enquiries
- cost of the automation and ongoing support

A process with high volume, clear rules and low judgement content will usually produce a cleaner payback than a complex process with many exceptions. That is why document reminders, booking administration and task creation often make better first projects than trying to automate a whole advisory engagement.

Security should be considered at the same time. If a workflow touches client documents, cloud accounts or email, make sure permissions, MFA, backup and escalation controls are understood before go-live. If management wants to know whether the change is working, report the baseline and post-launch measures in the same monthly dashboard.

This is the standard a CFO will respect: a defined problem, an owner, a cost, a measurable return and a control plan.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is accounting workflow automation?

It is the use of defined rules and connected software to move routine work between steps with fewer manual actions.

### Which accounting tasks are easiest to automate?

Enquiry acknowledgement, appointment reminders, document requests, recurring task creation and internal notifications are common starting points.

### Should every workflow be automated?

No. Processes requiring professional judgement, unusual interpretation or sensitive decisions should retain human control.

### How long should an automation project take?

Start with one narrow workflow. The important measure is not speed of installation but whether the process works reliably for the team and clients.

## Start with one process

Pick the workflow your team complains about most often. Map it, simplify it, define controls and then automate the repeatable parts.
