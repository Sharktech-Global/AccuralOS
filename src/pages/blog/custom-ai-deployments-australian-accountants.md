# Custom AI Deployments for Australian Accountants: Building Automation Around Your Practice

Off-the-shelf automation is valuable when the workflow is common. Custom AI becomes relevant when the business has a process that is commercially important, repetitive enough to justify investment and too specific for a standard configuration.

That can apply to accounting practices, tax teams, bookkeeping firms, CFO advisory businesses, finance managers and finance brokers. The technology should still begin with the process and economics, not with a desire to build something custom.

## What is a custom AI deployment for an accounting firm?

A custom AI deployment is an implementation designed around the accounting firm's own workflows, systems, controls and client journey.

It may combine AI assistants, workflow automation, CRM configuration, integrations, data routing, notifications, document handling and reporting.

The important word is custom.

The automation is built around the practice rather than asking the practice to change everything to suit the software.

## When does an accounting practice need a custom AI solution?

A standard setup may be enough for common workflows such as enquiry capture, appointment booking or basic client reminders.

A custom deployment becomes more relevant when a firm has:

- multiple service lines
- complex client onboarding
- different workflows for different client types
- unique compliance processes
- existing CRM or practice management systems
- specialist document requirements
- internal approval rules
- multiple accounting platforms
- reporting requirements across several systems
- legacy tools that still need to be part of the process

The first step is not development. It is process discovery.

## Start with process mapping

Before building an AI agent or automation, document the way the practice works today.

For each workflow, identify:

1. what starts the process
2. what information is required
3. who owns each step
4. which system holds the information
5. which actions are repetitive
6. which decisions require professional judgement
7. what can go wrong
8. when a person must take over
9. what the client should see
10. what result the practice wants to improve

This creates the deployment blueprint.

## Custom AI agents for accounting practices

A custom AI agent should have a narrow role and clear boundaries.

For example, an accounting practice might use an AI assistant to:

- classify incoming enquiries
- answer approved general questions
- collect preliminary information
- summarise prospect or client information
- prepare draft communications
- route requests to the right person
- identify missing information
- trigger a workflow
- prepare internal summaries
- support staff with approved knowledge

The AI assistant should not be treated as an unsupervised accountant.

Professional advice, material decisions and sensitive exceptions need appropriate human oversight.

## Integration with Xero, MYOB and QuickBooks

Most practices already have accounting software they trust.

The stronger automation model is usually to keep those systems and build better workflows around them.

For a practice using Xero, MYOB or QuickBooks, the deployment should define:

- what information needs to move between systems
- which system is the source of truth
- what actions can be triggered automatically
- what data must remain inside the accounting platform
- what staff approvals are required
- how duplicate data will be prevented
- what happens if an integration fails

This is why integration planning matters as much as the AI itself.

## When custom AI creates enterprise value

Custom work should have a higher approval threshold than standard configuration because the cost, support burden and implementation risk are greater. A CFO should expect a documented business case that covers annual effort, revenue impact, control improvement, build cost, ongoing support, dependencies and downside risk.

Examples of potentially defensible use cases include a specialist tax intake process, a high-volume document classification workflow. A finance-broking application triage process, an advisory practice's recurring board-pack preparation or a multi-system client onboarding flow. The use case must still be scoped against privacy, professional obligations and the capabilities of the source systems.

Use stage gates. First prove the process and data. Then build a limited pilot. Then measure adoption and results. Expand only when the evidence supports it.

A custom deployment also needs an owner after launch. Models, prompts, integrations and business rules can drift as the practice changes. Ongoing monitoring, change control and support should therefore be treated as part of total cost of ownership.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a custom AI deployment for accountants?

It is an AI and automation implementation designed around the specific workflows, systems, controls and client journey of an accounting practice.

### Can a custom AI solution work with Xero, MYOB or QuickBooks?

Potentially, yes. The exact integration depends on the systems, available APIs, permissions and the workflow being designed. These requirements should be confirmed during deployment scoping.

### Is custom AI only for large accounting firms?

No. A smaller practice may justify a custom deployment if it has a high-value repetitive process or a unique workflow that standard software does not handle well.

### Can Sharktech provide ongoing support after deployment?

A custom deployment should include an agreed support model covering monitoring, troubleshooting, changes and optimisation. The exact support scope should be defined in the engagement.

### Does AI replace the accountant?

No. The purpose is to automate repeatable operational work while keeping professional judgement and accountability with appropriately qualified people.
