# AI Deployment and IT Support for Australian Accountants: What Good Implementation Looks Like

The quality of an AI platform matters, but deployment quality usually determines whether the investment produces a return. Accounting practices need process discovery, integration planning, controls, training and support after go-live.

For a CFO, the evaluation should look less like a software demo and more like vendor due diligence.

## Start with the practice, not the tool

A provider should first understand how the firm handles enquiries, onboarding, documents, recurring work and client communication. Technology should then be configured around the agreed process.

## What deployment should include

A practical deployment should define the workflow, identify systems that need to connect, set human approval points. Test common and unusual scenarios, train users and establish who owns support after launch.

For accounting firms, data access and professional responsibility also need clear boundaries.

## IT support is part of adoption

A technically correct automation can still fail if staff do not understand it or cannot get help when something changes. Good support explains what happened, resolves issues, documents changes and helps the practice improve the workflow over time.

## A CFO due-diligence checklist for AI deployment

Before approving a provider, ask for the commercial and operating model in plain English. What is included in setup? What is recurring? Who owns configuration? How are integrations scoped? What training is provided? What happens when a workflow fails? What response can the practice expect from support?

Build total cost of ownership rather than looking only at subscription price. Include implementation, migration, staff time, training, additional connectors, support and any cybersecurity work required to make the deployment safe.

Then connect the spend to a measurable benefit. Useful measures include staff hours released, faster onboarding, improved enquiry conversion, lower rework, better WIP visibility and fewer overdue client actions.

Vendor risk matters too. Confirm data handling, permissions, backup, escalation, change control and how the provider works with the firm's existing IT company. Sharktech's cybersecurity services are deliberately separate from general helpdesk support, which makes the boundary clearer.

Australian support is valuable when it improves implementation and accountability. Sharktech operates from Sydney and Melbourne and supports practices nationally. The decision should still be based on fit, economics and support quality.

The strongest provider is not the one with the longest feature list. It is the one that can explain the business problem, deployment plan, controls, cost and expected result without hiding behind technical language.

## Ask for an implementation owner, not just a support inbox

A finance leader should know who is accountable for taking the project from discovery to stable operation. Generic support is useful after launch, but it does not replace implementation ownership during the change.

The owner should coordinate workflow design, testing, training, issue resolution and handover. They should also be able to explain open risks and what is required from the practice. That level of accountability makes it easier to control timing and cost. And it reduces the chance that unresolved decisions are pushed back onto busy accountants after the system is switched on.
