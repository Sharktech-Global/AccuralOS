# Analytics and Reporting for Australian Accounting Practices: Build the Management View Your Firm Actually Needs

Accounting firms help clients interpret financial information every day. Yet many practices still manage their own operations through spreadsheets, exports, inboxes and reports that do not reconcile cleanly.

That creates a management problem.

A partner wants to know whether WIP is rising. A practice manager wants to know which jobs are blocked. A CFO wants to understand cash conversion and debtor behaviour. A tax team wants to see workload by deadline. A bookkeeping team wants to understand client effort and margin.

The data often exists. The answer does not.

## Start with the management question

Good reporting begins with a business question, not a dashboard template.

Examples include:

- Why is cash conversion slower this quarter?
- Which service line is creating the most WIP?
- Which clients consume disproportionate staff time?
- Where are jobs sitting too long?
- How many enquiries become paying clients?
- Are we adding revenue faster than we are adding complexity?

The technology should support the answer.

## What should an accounting practice measure?

The right measures depend on the business model, but useful reporting may include revenue by service line, WIP and WIP ageing, lock-up, realisation, debtor ageing. Client profitability, recurring versus project revenue, enquiry conversion, onboarding time, workflow ageing, team capacity, write-offs, client concentration, cash collection and forecast versus actual performance.

The definitions matter as much as the visuals.

A dashboard that calculates realisation differently from the finance team is not useful, even if it looks polished.

## Reporting for CFOs and finance managers

A CFO or finance manager usually needs a broader management view.

That may include profit and loss, balance sheet, cash flow, budget versus actual, rolling forecast. Debtor ageing, working capital, headcount, revenue bridge, margin bridge, capital expenditure, risk and decisions required.

The strongest reporting architecture connects operating data and finance data instead of forcing management to reconcile them manually each month.

## Why this matters commercially

A reporting project should improve decision quality, reduce manual reporting effort or expose an operating issue early enough to act on it.

A CFO should ask what decision the report improves, what manual effort it replaces, which data sources are required. Who owns each KPI definition, how often the information must refresh and how the business will measure the benefit.

That is the difference between reporting as decoration and reporting as management infrastructure.
