# AI for Accounting Firms in Perth: From Enquiry to Onboarding Without the Manual Chasing

The enquiry-to-onboarding journey is one of the easiest places for an accounting practice to lose time. The prospect may be genuine, but the process stalls between emails, calendars, forms, documents and internal handovers.

For Perth firms, a well-designed workflow can reduce that friction without reducing the quality of the professional relationship.

## Map the client journey

Write down every step from first contact to active client. Identify who owns each step, what information is needed and where the process commonly stalls.

This exercise often reveals that the problem is not a lack of accounting software. It is the number of disconnected administrative actions around the client.

## Capture and qualify enquiries

A defined workflow can record the source of an enquiry, acknowledge it and collect basic approved information. High-value or unusual enquiries can be routed directly to a person.

## Simplify booking

Qualified prospects should have a clear way to schedule the appropriate conversation. Confirmations and reminders can be automated, while staff retain control of the actual consultation.

## Build onboarding as a workflow

Once a client proceeds, trigger a structured onboarding path. Track required information, documents, meetings and internal responsibilities.

The workflow should show what is complete, what remains outstanding and who needs to act.

## Use reminders without sounding robotic

Automation should be tied to real events. A missing document can trigger a useful reminder. Once the document arrives, the reminder stops.

That is better than sending generic messages simply because a marketing sequence says it is Tuesday.

## Measure the handover cost

The hidden cost in onboarding is often not one large task. It is the number of handovers. Every time information moves from inbox to spreadsheet, from spreadsheet to CRM. Or from one staff member to another, there is a delay and an opportunity for something to be missed.

A practice manager or CFO should measure:

- days from enquiry to first meeting
- days from acceptance to complete onboarding
- number of manual follow-ups
- percentage of onboarding items outstanding after an agreed period
- staff time spent coordinating rather than advising
- client drop-off before engagement

Automation should reduce the handovers, not create a new system that staff must update in parallel. That is why the source of truth and integration plan need to be clear.

If the workflow uses email, cloud documents or client portals, security belongs in the design. Access rules, MFA, email authentication and backup are more useful when reviewed before the workflow scales.

For management, the payoff is a cleaner pipeline, fewer stalled clients and better visibility over capacity. That is a more meaningful result than simply saying the practice has adopted AI.

## Do not automate a poor handover

If the existing onboarding process is unclear, automation can make the confusion happen faster. Before configuring technology, agree the minimum information required, the owner of each stage and the point at which a matter is ready to move forward.

That discipline is especially useful for tax and bookkeeping teams because incomplete information often creates repeated follow-up. Once the standard path is clear, the system can handle reminders and status changes while exceptions go to a person. Management then has a cleaner basis for measuring whether the workflow is reducing delay rather than merely generating more notifications.

## Questions to take into the partner or management meeting

Before committing to the change, management should be able to answer five practical questions. What problem are we solving? What does the process cost us today? Which result will show that the change worked? Which risks or approvals must remain under human control? Who owns the process after go-live?

Those questions keep the technology conversation grounded in capacity, cash, client service and control. They also make it easier to compare providers because the practice is evaluating an operating outcome rather than a feature list.
