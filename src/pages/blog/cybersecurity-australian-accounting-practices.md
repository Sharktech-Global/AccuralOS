# Cybersecurity for Australian Accounting Practices: Protecting the Systems Behind Client Service

Cybersecurity is a finance issue as much as a technology issue. A security event can interrupt billing, expose client information, redirect payments, consume management time and create legal, insurance and reputational consequences.

For accounting practices, tax agents, bookkeepers, CFO teams and finance brokers, the sensible approach is to understand the current exposure. Prioritise the highest-risk gaps and make security part of normal operating governance.

## Why cybersecurity belongs in an accounting automation plan

Accounting teams handle client information, invoices, payroll-related communications, tax documents and financial records. They also rely heavily on email and Microsoft 365 or other cloud services.

Automation can move information faster. Security controls help make sure that speed does not create unnecessary exposure.

A practical sequence is to understand the current environment, fix high-priority gaps, then automate with clearer permissions and controls.

## Start with a Security Health Check

Sharktech's Security Health Check reviews systems, accounts, devices and backups against the Essential Eight baseline published by the Australian Signals Directorate.

The defined scope includes maturity assessment across the eight strategies, user and administrator accounts, multi-factor authentication, patching, backups and recovery checks. The outcome is a written assessment and prioritised remediation roadmap.

For an accounting practice that has never had an independent security review, this can provide a useful baseline before expanding AI, CRM or workflow automation.

## Protect Microsoft 365 and cloud access

Cloud accounts are central to many accounting practices.

Sharktech's Cloud and Microsoft 365 Security Review looks at administrator accounts, dormant users, guest access, older sign-in methods, MFA, access rules, logging and backups. The service is designed to review the configuration and make agreed changes with the client.

That matters when a practice is connecting more workflows because identity and access controls sit underneath many automations.

## Reduce email and invoice-fraud risk

Email remains a major operational channel for accountants.

Sharktech's Email Security and Fraud Protection service covers SPF, DKIM and DMARC configuration. An audit of services sending mail as the firm's domain, mailbox forwarding and rule checks, and a payment-verification process.

For accounting practices, that is particularly relevant because staff may receive payment instructions, invoices and sensitive client communications every day.

## Train the people attackers target

Technology controls do not remove the need for staff awareness.

Security Awareness Training can include practical education on phishing, invoice fraud and password practices, followed by simulated phishing campaigns and trend reporting.

The objective is improvement, not blame.

## Test public-facing systems

Where a firm has a website, application, portal or client login. Authorised Vulnerability Assessment and Penetration Testing can identify exploitable issues and provide evidence, severity ratings and remediation steps.

Website and Web Application Security can also address certificates, security headers, patching, administrator access, firewall rules and tested backups.

## The CFO lens: quantify the business exposure

Security spending is easier to govern when it is tied to business impact. Start with the systems that support revenue, client data, payments and statutory deadlines. Ask how long the practice could operate if email, cloud files or a client portal became unavailable.

The finance leader should also understand payment-control risk. Email compromise and invoice fraud are not abstract cyber problems when they can result in money being sent to the wrong account. Email authentication, mailbox-rule reviews and a documented payment-verification process are therefore part of financial control.

A Security Health Check provides a practical baseline against the Essential Eight. Microsoft 365 and cloud reviews can strengthen identity and access. Staff awareness and phishing simulations test the human side. Penetration testing and web security are relevant where the practice exposes portals or applications. Policies and incident-response planning help the firm respond more consistently when insurers, tenders or larger clients ask for evidence.

Sharktech's monitoring is during Australian business hours, and its cybersecurity work is designed to complement rather than replace the client's general IT provider. That boundary should be clear in any engagement.

A CFO does not need to become a security engineer. The CFO does need to know the material risks, the controls in place, the owner of each action and whether priority gaps are actually being closed.
