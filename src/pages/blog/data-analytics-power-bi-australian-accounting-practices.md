# Data Analytics for Australian Accounting Practices: From Spreadsheets to Better Practice Visibility

An accounting practice can produce excellent financial reports for clients and still have weak visibility over its own operating performance. WIP, lock-up, utilisation, revenue, pipeline and client data may sit in different systems with different definitions.

For a CFO or practice leader, the problem is not a shortage of data. It is the time required to turn that data into a reliable management view.

## What can data analytics change for an accounting practice?

The biggest difference is visibility.

Instead of waiting for static month-end exports, a practice can use Power BI dashboards built around the decisions directors and managers need to make.

For accounting practices, examples may include:

- WIP and WIP ageing
- lock-up
- realisation
- revenue trends
- client mix
- workflow or service-line performance
- enquiry and onboarding activity where the relevant data is available
- operational KPIs agreed by the practice

The exact dashboard should follow the data that actually exists and the decisions the firm needs to make.

## Data strategy, governance and quality

Two people should not be presenting different numbers for the same KPI because they used different spreadsheets.

Data strategy and governance can establish definitions, access rules and validation checks so the practice has a clearer version of the truth.

For an accounting firm, that can make internal management conversations faster and reduce time spent debating which report is correct.

## How this can make a difference for Australian accountants

A useful analytics implementation can help a practice:

1. reduce manual spreadsheet consolidation
2. establish consistent KPI definitions
3. see operational trends earlier
4. give directors and managers a shared view
5. identify where further automation may have the greatest value

That creates a feedback loop between data and automation.

**Measure the workflow to identify the bottleneck to automate the right step to measure the result.**

## Build the dashboard around decisions, not available data

Start with the monthly questions management actually needs to answer. Is WIP ageing? Is lock-up improving? Which service lines are growing? Where are write-offs occurring? Is the pipeline sufficient for planned capacity? Are debtor and cash trends moving in the right direction?

The dashboard should then be designed around those decisions. Useful measures can include revenue by service line, gross margin where available, WIP ageing, lock-up, realisation, debtor ageing, enquiry conversion, client mix and staff capacity. The exact set depends on the firm's business model and data quality.

A CFO should insist on clear KPI definitions. If two partners calculate realisation differently, a beautiful dashboard will only make the disagreement faster. Data governance therefore comes before visualisation.

Sharktech's Data Analytics service uses Power BI, built-in connectors and Power Query for its standard connection model, with data strategy, governance and quality support. It can also provide managed analytics and selected classical machine-learning work where there is a defined use case and suitable data.

The commercial value is faster, more consistent decision-making. A dashboard that does not change a decision, trigger an action or reduce reporting effort is decoration. A useful dashboard tells management where cash, capacity or client delivery needs attention.
