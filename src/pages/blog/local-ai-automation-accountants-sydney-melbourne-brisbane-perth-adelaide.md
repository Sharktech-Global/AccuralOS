## What national support should look like to a finance leader

A CFO or practice owner should define the support model before go-live. Who handles workflow questions? Who handles integration issues? What stays with the existing IT provider? How are urgent problems escalated? When are changes tested before release?

For multi-location practices, consistency matters. A workflow should not be configured differently in each office unless there is a genuine business reason. Common process, common data definitions and common reporting make management easier.

Sharktech's physical offices are in Sydney and Melbourne, with national support for Australian clients. Brisbane, Perth and Adelaide practices should be described as supported locations, not as office locations.

The wider service capability can also be used selectively. A multi-office firm may need a Microsoft 365 security review, phishing-awareness program or Power BI practice dashboard. A smaller firm may need only an AccrualOS workflow and a clear support path.

The finance test is simple: does the service model reduce operating friction and give management confidence that issues will be owned when they occur?

## Multi-location firms need one governance model

Where a practice operates across more than one city, decide which workflow rules are national and which genuinely need local variation. Too much local customisation increases support cost, weakens reporting consistency and makes future changes harder.

A CFO should approve exceptions deliberately. Common client stages, security standards, data definitions and management KPIs should be consistent unless there is a clear business reason to differ. This creates a cleaner foundation for national reporting and reduces the risk that each office develops its own version of the operating system.
